Early detection of oral cancer from risk to coverage: published results, my work and my research questions

The steps follow the order in which a person passes through them. Each step gives one published result or one result of my own work, and states what I did or what I propose to study.

Risk: who is at risk of oral cancer

70 to 80% of oral cancer cases in India are attributable to tobacco and areca nut use, according to Francis and Reddy (2026), who cite earlier research.

Source: Francis and Reddy, ecancermedicalscience, 2026.

What I did at this stepMy BDS research assessed the prevalence of tobacco use and associated oral lesions among non-teaching staff of a health care university.

Screening: examination of people without symptoms

0.66 rate ratio for oral cancer mortality among people who used tobacco, alcohol or both: intervention group versus control group in the Kerala trial (95% CI 0.45 to 0.95). A rate ratio below 1 means a lower mortality rate in the intervention group.

Source: Sankaranarayanan and colleagues, The Lancet, 2005.

What I did at this stepI ran oral cancer screenings in camps for industrial workers in Gujarat, India.

Referral: from a positive screening result to a specialist

63% of screen-positive participants in the Kerala trial complied with referral (3,218 of 5,145).

Source: Sankaranarayanan and colleagues, The Lancet, 2005.

What I did at this stepAt Madhuram Dental Care I examined patients for oral lesions and referred them where needed.

Research question I propose to studyIn workplace screening of adults who use tobacco, how many oral potentially malignant disorders are detected, and what proportion of screen-positive participants attend the referral?

Diagnosis: the stage at which the cancer is found

30 of 100 cases of invasive cancer of the oral cavity and pharynx were localised at diagnosis (SEER registries, United States, diagnoses 2007 to 2013). Five-year relative survival was 83.7 percent for localised and 38.5 percent for distant disease. Survival by stage does not by itself show the effect of earlier detection.

Source: SEER Cancer Statistics Review 1975 to 2014, Table 20.10.

Research question I propose to studyWhat proportion of oral cavity cancers are first suspected at a dental visit, and does the stage at diagnosis differ between patients with and without a dental visit in the preceding year?

Coverage: who pays for the examination

6 European countries: my MSc dissertation compared how their health systems cover dental treatment.

Source: MSc dissertation, Berlin School of Business and Innovation, 2024.

Research question I propose to studyIn the 6 countries compared in my MSc dissertation, is an examination of the oral mucosa part of the publicly covered dental check-up, and what does the patient pay for it?

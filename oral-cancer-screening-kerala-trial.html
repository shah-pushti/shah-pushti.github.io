Oral visual screening and deaths from oral cancer: results of the Kerala cluster-randomised trial

One cluster-randomised controlled trial has measured the effect of oral visual screening on oral cancer mortality. In the whole study population the difference was not statistically significant. Among people who used tobacco, alcohol or both, the oral cancer mortality rate was lower in the intervention group than in the control group.

Design: 13 clusters, three screening rounds, 1996 to 2004

Investigators from the International Agency for Research on Cancer and the Regional Cancer Centre in Trivandrum randomised 13 clusters in Trivandrum district, Kerala, India. Seven clusters formed the intervention group (96,517 eligible people) and six the control group (95,356). Eligible people were aged 35 or older and had no history of oral cancer.

In the intervention clusters, trained health workers offered oral visual inspection in three rounds at intervals of three years. 87,655 people (91 percent) were screened at least once and 29,102 (30 percent) three times. The analysis compared the clusters as randomised (intention to treat).

Results: oral cancer mortality in the intervention and control groups

The trial reports the oral cancer mortality rate in each group and the ratio of the two rates. A rate ratio below 1 means a lower rate in the intervention group. The 95% confidence interval (CI) gives the range of rate ratios compatible with the data. If it includes 1, the difference is not statistically significant.

In the whole study population the mortality rate was 16.4 per 100,000 person-years in the intervention group (77 deaths) and 20.7 in the control group (87 deaths). The difference was not statistically significant.

Among people who used tobacco, alcohol or both, the rates were 29.9 (70 deaths) and 45.4 (85 deaths), a rate ratio of 0.66. In male users the rate ratio was 0.57 (0.35 to 0.93). In female users it was 0.78 (0.43 to 1.42), an interval that includes 1.

Compliance with referral and diagnoses among screen-positive participants

5,145 people screened positive and 3,218 (63 percent) complied with referral. Among those, 2,252 had oral precancerous lesions (the trial's term; now classified as oral potentially malignant disorders), 131 had invasive oral cancer and 835 had benign lesions or normal findings. 37 percent of screen-positive people did not attend the referral.

Limitations and applicability to other populations

The trial authors concluded that oral visual screening can reduce oral cancer mortality in people who use tobacco or alcohol. Among people who used neither, there were 7 oral cancer deaths in the intervention group and 2 in the control group (rate ratio 3.47, 95% CI 0.12 to 96.51).

The trial was carried out in one region where tobacco chewing is common. It does not show whether the result holds in populations with lower incidence. The article on case numbers gives incidence rates by country.

A Cochrane review (2013) included this trial as the only randomised trial of oral cancer screening and rated it at high risk of bias.

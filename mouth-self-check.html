Mouth self-examination in seven steps, and the symptoms a dentist or doctor should examine

Before you start

Wash your hands. Use a mirror and good light.

The steps follow the Mouth Cancer Foundation self-check as reproduced by Cardiff and Vale University Health Board (NHS Wales). Face and neck are combined into one step here.

The seven steps

Each step names an area of the face or mouth, what to do and what to look or feel for.

Symptoms for which the NHS advises seeing a GP or dentist

The NHS advises seeing a GP or dentist if you have any of these symptoms:

  • a mouth ulcer that has lasted more than 3 weeks
  • a lump in your mouth, on your lip, on your neck or in your throat
  • a red or white patch in your mouth
  • pain in your mouth that is not going away
  • difficulty swallowing or speaking
  • a hoarse voice that does not go away

The NHS adds that these symptoms are very common and can be caused by other conditions. Having them does not definitely mean you have mouth cancer.

Self-examination and examination by a dentist

A self-examination helps you notice a change in your mouth. A dentist or doctor examines the change and decides whether you need further tests.

The evidence on fewer deaths from oral cancer presented on this site comes from screening by trained health workers. The article on the Kerala trial describes this evidence.

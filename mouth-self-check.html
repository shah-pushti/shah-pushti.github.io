Mouth self-examination in seven steps, and the symptoms a dentist or doctor should examine

This article shows a mouth self-examination in seven steps and lists the symptoms for which the NHS advises an examination.

A self-examination is a way to notice a change in your mouth. A dentist or doctor then examines the change.

The seven steps (adapted from the Mouth Cancer Foundation self-check)

The steps follow the Mouth Cancer Foundation self-check as reproduced by Cardiff and Vale University Health Board (NHS Wales). Face and neck are combined into one step here. Wash your hands. Use a mirror and good light. Select a step in the list or a number in the diagram.

Symptoms for which the NHS advises seeing a GP or dentist

  • a mouth ulcer that has lasted more than 3 weeks
  • a lump in your mouth, on your lip, on your neck or in your throat
  • a red or white patch in your mouth
  • pain in your mouth that is not going away
  • difficulty swallowing or speaking
  • a hoarse voice that does not go away

The NHS adds that these symptoms are very common and can be caused by other conditions. Having them does not definitely mean you have mouth cancer.

Limits of self-examination

A self-examination does not replace an examination by a dentist or doctor. This article does not cite evidence that self-examination leads to earlier diagnosis or fewer deaths. The evidence on screening by trained health workers is described in the article on the Kerala trial.

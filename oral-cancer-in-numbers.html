Oral cancer: estimated new cases by country and five-year survival by stage at diagnosis

Two sets of figures: estimated new cases of lip and oral cavity cancer by country in 2022, and five-year relative survival by the stage at which the cancer was diagnosed. A closing section explains how to read them.

Oral cancer in this article means cancer of the lip and oral cavity, the category counted by GLOBOCAN. The survival figures from the United States and from Germany also include the pharynx (throat). This is stated where they appear.

Estimated new cases and deaths by country, 2022

GLOBOCAN 2022, published by the International Agency for Research on Cancer, estimates 389,846 new cases of lip and oral cavity cancer and 188,438 deaths worldwide. An estimated 143,759 of the cases and 79,979 of the deaths occurred in India. That is more than one in three cases worldwide.

Case counts follow population size. Ranked by age-standardised incidence rate, the same source places Papua New Guinea highest (17.5 per 100,000), followed by Bangladesh (10.3), India (9.9), Pakistan (9.2) and Sri Lanka (8.1). The world rate is 4.0.

Francis and Reddy (2026), citing earlier research, state that approximately 70 to 80 percent of oral cancer cases in India are attributable to tobacco and areca nut use.

Five-year relative survival by stage at diagnosis

The SEER cancer registries in the United States report survival for invasive cancer of the oral cavity and pharynx together. For diagnoses from 2007 to 2013, five-year relative survival was 83.7 percent for localised disease, 64.2 percent for regional disease and 38.5 percent for distant disease. For all stages together it was 64.5 percent.

Relative survival is the observed survival of the patients divided by the survival expected in people of the same age and sex in the general population.

Germany: new cases and five-year relative survival

The Robert Koch Institute estimated about 13,900 new cases of cancer of the lip, oral cavity and pharynx (ICD-10 C00 to C14) in Germany for 2016. A national analysis of registry data (Jansen and colleagues, 2021) reports five-year relative survival of 53 percent in men and 63 percent in women for 2015 to 2017. Survival rose between 1999 to 2002 and 2013 to 2017, while the stage distribution at diagnosis stayed the same.

How to read these figures

The figures from the United States and Germany count cancers of the oral cavity and the pharynx together. They describe groups of patients diagnosed in the stated years, in one country each, and give averages for those groups rather than the outlook for one person.

Survival is counted from the date of diagnosis. A cancer found earlier therefore shows a longer survival time even if the date of death stays the same, an effect called lead-time bias. For this reason the effect of screening on deaths from oral cancer is measured in randomised trials. The article on the Kerala trial describes the one randomised trial of oral cancer screening.

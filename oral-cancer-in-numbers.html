Oral cancer: estimated new cases by country and five-year survival by stage at diagnosis

This article gives two sets of figures: estimated new cases of lip and oral cavity cancer by country in 2022, and five-year relative survival by stage at diagnosis. It also states what these figures do not show.

In this article, oral cancer means cancer of the lip and oral cavity, the category used by GLOBOCAN. Two sources below use a wider category that includes the pharynx (throat). Each is named where it is used.

Estimated new cases and deaths by country, 2022

GLOBOCAN 2022, published by the International Agency for Research on Cancer, estimates 389,846 new cases of lip and oral cavity cancer and 188,438 deaths worldwide. An estimated 143,759 of the cases and 79,979 of the deaths occurred in India. That is more than one in three cases worldwide.

Case counts depend on population size. By age-standardised incidence rate, the same source ranks Papua New Guinea highest (17.5 per 100,000), followed by Bangladesh (10.3), India (9.9), Pakistan (9.2) and Sri Lanka (8.1). The world rate is 4.0.

Francis and Reddy (2026), citing earlier research, state that approximately 70 to 80 percent of oral cancer cases in India are attributable to tobacco and areca nut use.

Five-year relative survival by stage at diagnosis (SEER, diagnoses 2007 to 2013)

The SEER registries in the United States report survival for invasive cancer of the oral cavity and pharynx together. For diagnoses from 2007 to 2013, five-year relative survival was 83.7 percent for localised disease, 64.2 percent for regional disease and 38.5 percent for distant disease. For all stages together it was 64.5 percent.

Relative survival is the observed survival of the patients divided by the survival expected in people of the same age and sex in the general population.

In the same table, 30 percent of cases were localised at diagnosis, 47 percent regional, 19 percent distant and 4 percent unstaged.

Germany: new cases and five-year relative survival (lip, oral cavity and pharynx)

The Robert Koch Institute estimated about 13,900 new cases of cancer of the lip, oral cavity and pharynx (ICD-10 C00 to C14) in Germany for 2016. A national analysis of registry data (Jansen and colleagues, 2021) reports five-year relative survival of 53 percent in men and 63 percent in women for 2015 to 2017. Survival rose between 1999 to 2002 and 2013 to 2017. The stage distribution at diagnosis did not change.

What these figures do not show

The SEER and German figures cover the oral cavity and the pharynx together. They do not show how often cancer of the oral cavity alone is diagnosed at a localised stage. The SEER figures refer to diagnoses from 2007 to 2013 in one country. They do not predict the outcome for any one person.

Survival by stage does not show the effect of earlier detection. An earlier diagnosis lengthens measured survival even when the date of death does not change (lead-time bias). Whether screening reduces deaths from oral cancer has to be tested in a trial. The article on the Kerala trial describes the one randomised trial.
